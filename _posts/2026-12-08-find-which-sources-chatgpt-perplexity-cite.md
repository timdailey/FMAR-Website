To find the sources AI tools cite in your industry, run the questions your buyers ask on each platform, record every domain that is linked in the answers, and count which domains appear again and again. Those repeat domains are where AI tools already look, which makes them your target list.

The method below is free, takes about two hours the first time, and gets quicker after that. It works for any category.

## Step 1: Write 10 to 15 buyer questions

Write the questions as a buyer would type them into an AI tool, not as keywords. Mix three types:

- **Discovery:** "What are the best tools for managing supplier contracts at a mid-size company?"
- **Comparison:** "How does [category A] compare with [category B] for a team of ten?"
- **Evaluation:** "What should I look for when choosing a [type of provider]?"

Keep the list fixed once you start. Changing the wording makes later comparisons unreliable. The [tracking guide]({{ '/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility/' | relative_url }}) has more on building a query set and an example list.

## Step 2: Run each question in a fresh session

Use a new chat or search for every question so earlier answers don't shape the next one. Run the set on:

- **Perplexity.** Each answer includes numbered citations that link to the original sources, according to Perplexity's [help center](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352895-how-does-perplexity-work). Open the numbered sources to see the domains.
- **ChatGPT with search.** When it uses the web, the answer includes links to the pages it drew on. The layout and controls change, so check the current interface. If an answer has no links, record "no sources" and note that it likely came from training data. For why that matters, see [where ChatGPT gets its information]({{ '/blog/where-does-chatgpt-get-its-information/' | relative_url }}).
- **Google AI Overviews.** Where an overview appears, it shows links alongside the summary. Google calls these supporting links, and notes that AI Overviews do not appear for every query ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)).

Use a private or logged-out window where you can, and expect some personalization anyway. That is why the next steps look for patterns and not single results.

## Step 3: Log the results

Copy this into a spreadsheet, one row per question per platform:

```
question, platform, date, brand_mentioned (Y/N), cited_domains (semicolon separated), competitors_named, notes
```

For each answer, record:

- Whether your brand is named in the text.
- Every domain that is linked or cited.
- The competitors named in the answer.
- Whether the answer had any sources at all.

Record the domain (for example, `g2.com`), not just the page, so the counts add up in the next step.

## Step 4: Count the repeat domains

Pivot or sort the sheet by domain. You are looking for two numbers per domain: how many questions it appears on, and how many platforms it appears on. A domain that shows up on six questions across all three platforms matters more than one that appears once.

Then sort each repeat domain into a type:

| Type | Examples | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Review sites and directories | G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, industry directories | Your profile there is likely a high-leverage signal |
| Media and trade publications | Industry news sites, analyst blogs | Coverage there reaches the sources AI tools read |
| Communities and forums | Reddit, Quora, niche forums | Buyer conversations are being used as evidence |
| Vendor and competitor sites | Your competitors' own blogs and guides | Their content is answering the questions you want |
| Reference sources | Wikipedia, Wikidata, standards bodies | Entity verification happens here |
| Your own site | Your domain | Which of your pages are already being used |

## Step 5: Turn the gaps into actions

For each repeat domain, ask what you could do there.

- **Directories and review sites:** Claim and complete your profile, and keep the name, description, and category consistent with your other profiles. [Entity signals]({{ '/glossary/entity-signals/' | relative_url }}) lists the profiles that matter most.
- **Publications:** Make a short list of the publications that keep appearing and the writers who cover your category. Pitch them something useful, such as original data or an expert comment, not a request for a link.
- **Communities:** Join the threads where your category is discussed and give useful answers under your own name, following each community's rules.
- **Competitor content:** Read the cited pages and note what they cover that your pages don't. Write a better, better-sourced answer to the same question.
- **Your own pages:** If a question names competitors but not you, and you have a page on the topic, check it against the [AI citation checklist]({{ '/blog/ai-citation-checklist/' | relative_url }}).

Don't try to do everything. Pick the two or three domains that repeat most, and work on those first.

## Step 6: Re-run every month

Run the same questions on the same day each month, log the results, and compare. Change things between runs in batches and write down what you changed. If the same domains keep appearing and your brand starts to appear on them, that is a useful signal. If it doesn't move after a few cycles, revisit the basics in [why your website isn't showing up in AI answers]({{ '/blog/why-website-not-showing-in-ai-answers/' | relative_url }}).

## What to keep in mind

- **One run is a snapshot.** AI outputs differ between sessions, so rely on patterns across many questions.
- **A cited domain is a lead, not a rule.** AI tools change how they choose sources, and a site that is cited today may not be next quarter.
- **Mentions and citations are different.** A brand mention is your name in the answer. A sourced citation is a link to a page. Track both separately. [What an AI citation is]({{ '/glossary/what-is-an-ai-citation/' | relative_url }}) explains the difference.
