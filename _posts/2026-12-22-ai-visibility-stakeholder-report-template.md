faq:
  - q: What should an AI visibility report for executives include?
    a: Headline metrics (citation rate, Share of AI Voice, and accuracy), the change since last month, a breakdown by platform, a comparison with named competitors, the actions you took, the next actions, and a short note on limits. Keep it to one page.
  - q: How often should I report on AI visibility?
    a: Monthly is a sensible cadence for most teams. It leaves time to make changes and see whether they had an effect, and it avoids reading too much into the variation between sessions that AI answers show.
  - q: How do I explain uncertainty to executives?
    a: State once, near the top, that AI answers vary between sessions, so the numbers are point-in-time measurements and trends matter more than any single month. Then report direction over three or more months instead of precise percentages.
  - q: Should the report show mentions or citations?
    a: Say which one you mean. A mention is your brand name appearing in an answer. A citation, in the narrower sense, is a linked reference to a page. They measure different things, and mixing them overstates results.
  - q: What is a good citation rate to aim for?
    a: We don't know of a verified benchmark that applies across industries. Compare your rate with the competitors you track on the same questions, and set a goal as a change from your own baseline.
  - q: How do I tie AI visibility to business results?
    a: Be careful. Referral traffic from AI tools is still small for most sites, and attribution is imperfect. Report AI-referral sessions and any leads you can trace, and describe visibility metrics as leading indicators, not as revenue.

An AI visibility report for executives should fit on one page and answer four questions: where are we now, what changed since last month, how do we compare with competitors, and what are we doing next. This post gives you a template for it, a worked example with made-up numbers, and guidance on how to describe uncertainty without losing the room.

## What a stakeholder report needs to do

Executives don't need the full data. They need to know whether the brand is more or less visible in AI answers than before, whether it is ahead of or behind competitors, and what the team plans to do. They also need a clear warning that these numbers move around, so they don't read a bad month as a failure or a good month as a win.

The metrics come from the tracking process described in [how to track your brand's AI visibility]({{ '/blog/how-to-track-ai-visibility/' | relative_url }}): a fixed set of buyer questions, run each month in fresh sessions on each platform. If you have not set that up yet, start there.

## The one-page template

Copy this into a document and fill it in each month.

```
AI VISIBILITY REPORT: [Month Year]
Prepared by: [name]   Question set: [N] questions, unchanged since [month]

1. SUMMARY (two or three sentences)
   [What changed, in plain language. Example: "Citation rate rose on
   Perplexity and was flat on ChatGPT. We are still behind Competitor A."]

2. HEADLINE METRICS            This month   Last month   3 months ago
   Citation rate (all)         [ ]%         [ ]%         [ ]%
   Share of AI Voice           [ ]%         [ ]%         [ ]%
   Accuracy (accurate answers) [ ]%         [ ]%         [ ]%

3. BY PLATFORM
   Platform         Citation rate   Change vs. last month
   ChatGPT          [ ]%            [+/- ] pts
   Perplexity       [ ]%            [+/- ] pts
   Claude           [ ]%            [+/- ] pts
   Google AI Overviews  [ ]%        [+/- ] pts

4. VS. COMPETITORS (same questions)
   Brand            Citation rate   Share of AI Voice
   Us               [ ]%            [ ]%
   Competitor A     [ ]%            [ ]%
   Competitor B     [ ]%            [ ]%

5. WHAT WE CHANGED LAST MONTH
   - [Action, date]
   - [Action, date]

6. WHAT WE ARE DOING NEXT MONTH
   - [Action, owner]

7. RISKS AND CAVEATS
   - AI answers vary between sessions; trends over 3+ months matter more
     than any single month.
   - Citation rate counts brand mentions, not linked sources.
   - [Any model or platform changes that affect comparison]

8. TRAFFIC AND LEADS (if available)
   AI-referral sessions: [ ]   Leads traced to AI referrals: [ ]
```

## A worked example

The numbers below are **illustrative only. They are invented to show the format, not real data and not benchmarks.**

**Summary:** Citation rate rose on Perplexity and was flat elsewhere. We remain behind Competitor A, but the gap narrowed. Changes made in October may take another cycle to show up.

| Metric | This month | Last month | 3 months ago |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citation rate (all platforms) | 30% | 25% | 20% |
| Share of AI Voice | 23% | 20% | 17% |
| Accurate answers, when named | 80% | 75% | 70% |

| Platform | Citation rate | Change vs. last month |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT | 20% | 0 pts |
| Perplexity | 50% | +10 pts |
| Claude | 30% | +5 pts |
| Google AI Overviews | 20% | 0 pts |

| Brand | Citation rate | Share of AI Voice |
|---|---|---|
| Us | 30% | 23% |
| Competitor A | 60% | 46% |
| Competitor B | 40% | 31% |

**What we changed:** claimed and completed two directory profiles; added Organization and FAQPage schema to the product pages.

**Next month:** publish two comparison pages; request reviews on one site where competitors are consistently cited.

The Share of AI Voice figures follow the calculation in [what Share of AI Voice is]({{ '/glossary/share-of-ai-voice/' | relative_url }}): your count divided by the combined count of all tracked brands on the same question set. Keep the competitor set fixed, since the denominator changes when it does.

## How to talk about uncertainty

Say it once, near the top, in a sentence. For example:

> AI answers differ from one session to the next, so these figures are point-in-time measurements. We focus on direction over several months, not on any single month.

Then:

- **Report direction, not precision.** "Up from 20% to 30% over three months" is more honest than "up 3.2 points."
- **Don't over-explain.** A short note beats a long methodology section. Put the method in an appendix or link to the tracking guide.
- **Flag platform changes.** If a model or product update changes how a platform answers, note it so the change isn't blamed on your work.
- **Avoid claiming causes you can't show.** If you made three changes in a month and the rate rose, say the rate rose after those changes, not because of them.

## Metrics to be careful with

- **Mentions vs. citations.** Citation rate in this template means brand mentions. A sourced citation is a linked reference to a specific page. [What an AI citation is]({{ '/glossary/what-is-an-ai-citation/' | relative_url }}) explains the difference. Label which one you report.
- **Aggregate scores.** A blended number hides the platform split, which is where the specific fixes are. Always show the per-platform table.
- **Revenue claims.** AI referral traffic is still a small share of site traffic in the studies we have seen, and attribution is imperfect. Present AI visibility as a leading indicator and show traffic and leads separately.
